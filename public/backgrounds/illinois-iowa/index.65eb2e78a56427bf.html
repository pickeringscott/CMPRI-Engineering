    About this theme

    Built on the supplied GiesOverheadView.jpg. An illustrated four-quarter simulation, not a live broadcast. All 60,670 seats are filled: 33,369 Illinois fans and 27,301 Iowa fans. Iowa occupies the section 109 core in the upper right and blends outward along both neighboring seating arms; Illinois occupies the remaining home sections. Iowa is labeled AP No. 15. Illinois has no rank label on the scorebug. The broadcast scorebug at bottom center shows the simulated score, clock, quarter, and down. Every completed play appears in the bottom ticker and play-by-play log. The completed-game win/loss tally persists locally. The second half starts with the opposite opening receiver. Two-minute warnings in the second and fourth quarters and halftime have explicit clock-stopped intermissions. Mobile portrait artwork is vertical, with an upright scorebug fixed at the bottom of the screen. Artwork uses WebP compression, and a versioned IndexedDB cache stores the rendered scene plus compressed animation metadata. Stationary sideline figures are baked into the background. Only the field players and a small set of moving staff and fans are redrawn, using cached sprites at up to 15 frames per second in Low CPU mode, or 24 in Smooth mode. HUD values are updated only when they change. The moving canvas follows the actual display size, redraws only changed areas, and stops when hidden or offscreen. New-game preparation yields between short batches and reuses its frame storage. The scorebug and score numerals are smaller. Low CPU mode is the default; it keeps sidelines visible on a separate static layer while the game moves. Smooth mode also animates a few staff and fans. The motion choice is saved locally. If browser storage is unavailable the embedded file still runs offline. Each new game is sampled from randomized player interactions, with at least three lead changes. The game plays individual snaps with downs, distance, field position, first downs, punts, field goals, sacks, incompletions, interceptions, touchdowns, and changing possession. Player movement, the airborne ball, catches and contact resolve each snap; the rules engine carries that result into the next down. Playback speed is selectable. Neither team is scripted to win; ties are resampled. Both sidelines are filled with reserve players, seated bench groups, headset coaches, trainers, medical tables, coolers, and media crews. Field players use independent role-specific routes, pass protection, run blocking, coverage, pursuit, and tackles. Theme view presents the full artwork without the preview chrome. Motion pauses when the page is hidden and starts paused if your device requests reduced motion. This standalone preview follows CMPRI’s shared-background, responsive-layout, keyboard-focus, and motion-control requirements. CMPRI integration uses the shared sign-in/desktop background registry, shared desktop palette, and versioned asset cache.
